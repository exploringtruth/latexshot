Screenshot an equation. Copy it as LaTeX.

Draw a box around a printed equation in a PDF or website. LaTeXShot reads it on your Mac and copies the LaTeX, ready to paste.

Download for Mac First-launch instructions

Apple silicon · macOS 14 or later

From screen to source

  1. Capture

    Press ⌘⇧L or choose Capture Equation in the menu bar. Drag around the formula. Press Escape to cancel.

  2. Recognize

    The bundled Pix2Text model reads the selected region offline. A popup previews the equation when it can render it.

  3. Paste

    Paste the result into a document, notebook or editor. Your preferred clipboard format is applied when LaTeXShot copies it.

Make it fit your work

Keep equations within reach

Open Equation History from the menu bar. Click a rendered equation to copy it again, or drag it into a compatible text field. Both use the current clipboard format.

History keeps your last 10 copies by default and can hold up to 5,000. Pause recording in Settings or clear saved entries from the History panel.

Choose what goes on the clipboard

Use raw LaTeX, Markdown, LaTeX delimiters, or a custom prefix and suffix. Custom text keeps spaces and line breaks.

Changing the format in Settings → General recopies the latest equation when one is available.

Recognition stays
on your Mac.

Equation crops stay in memory while the bundled model reads them. Neither crops nor equations are uploaded. Your preferences and equation history stay on your Mac.

LaTeXShot uses Screen Recording to read the screen region you select. It has no analytics. Feedback opens GitHub Issues in your browser.

Install LaTeXShot

For Apple silicon Macs running macOS 14 or later.

Download for Mac Installer checksum ZIP and release notes
  1. Open the DMG and drag LaTeXShot into Applications.
  2. Open LaTeXShot and look for its icon in the menu bar.
  3. Grant Screen Recording permission when macOS asks.

v1.0.0 is ad-hoc signed and unnotarized. Follow the first-launch instructions if macOS blocks it.

Questions

Which equations can it recognize?

LaTeXShot v1.0.0 supports printed equations in PDFs and websites. Handwriting support is planned. The frozen printed-equation benchmark accepted 60 of 66 results (90.9%), below the 95% target. Review the copied LaTeX before using it. See the benchmark results.

Do I need an internet connection?

The installed app recognizes equations entirely offline. Downloading the app or an update and opening GitHub for feedback requires an internet connection.

What if macOS blocks the first launch?

v1.0.0 is ad-hoc signed and unnotarized. If you trust your download, open System Settings → Privacy & Security and use Open Anyway. Read Apple’s instructions.

How do I update v1.0.0?

Download a newer version from GitHub Releases, quit LaTeXShot and replace it in Applications. In-app update checks and automatic updates are disabled in v1.0.0.
